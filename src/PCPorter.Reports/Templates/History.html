Web history

How to read this report Every row comes from a web browser's own history files, copied and read without starting the browser. A visit means the browser recorded opening that address at that time; it does not prove a person looked at it (pages can open themselves, and other people may use the same account). Browsers delete old history on their own, and private windows leave almost nothing. Addresses are shown as text and are never opened from this page. Secrets in addresses (passwords, tokens) are masked.

Visits that the browser's history list does not show: found in leftover data inside the history file (its free space, or earlier versions of its pages) or in older copies of the file kept in restore points. Leftover data does not prove a deletion: a row may have been deleted (history cleared or expired), or be an older copy of a visit the browser moved or changed. Method and limits

  • Read from: Chromium-family browsers (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, Chromium) and Firefox. Each history database was copied together with its not-yet-saved changes (the write-ahead log) into the case's private work folder and read from the copy. The originals were not changed.
  • Also read: bookmarks, the open and recently closed tabs from the browser's session files, and saved form entries (only the browser's form-history list; the form entries section stays closed until you open it).
  • Never read: saved passwords, cookies, encryption keys, payment cards, bank details and saved addresses. Form fields that look like passwords, card, PIN or account numbers are dropped, and so are values that look like a card number.
  • Chrome-family browsers keep about 90 days of visits; Firefox keeps visits until its history file reaches a size limit. Older visits are gone unless they are recovered from leftover data or a restore point.
  • Firefox keeps no separate list of searches: searches are recognised from the addresses of search result pages.
  • Private (InPrivate / Incognito) windows leave almost no history. A missing visit does not prove a site was never opened.
  • Times are recorded by the browser in UTC and shown here in the time zone of the computer showing this page. The CSV export contains UTC times.
  • "Recovered" rows come from leftover data inside the browser's files (free space, or earlier versions of pages kept in the write-ahead log; changes the browser had not finished saving are skipped) or from older copies in restore points; they are marked in the Origin column. A recovered row may have been deleted, or be an older copy of a visit: it is not proof of a deletion.
  • "Recovered (restore point)" visits are in a restore point's copy of a profile but not in that profile's current history. "Restore point copy" visits come from a profile whose current history could not be read, or that is no longer on the PC, so they could not be compared and may still be in the browser's history.
  • "From a damaged copy" rows come from a history file that could not be opened (damaged, or changing while it was copied); they were read from its raw bytes and are mostly visits the browser still lists.
